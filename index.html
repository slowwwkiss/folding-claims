Trust model — read this before you trust it

The oracle is the single trusted component. A Bitcoin Cash contract cannot read the Folding@home API, so someone must carry the score on-chain. That someone is the oracle: it reads api.foldingathome.org, computes your reward, and signs the claim. This is exactly the point FoldingCash's own whitepaper (§5.1.1) makes — any on-chain distribution still needs an oracle for Folding@home points.

  • What the oracle CANNOT do: steal escrowed funds (the contract also requires your signature), redirect your payout (the contract pins the output to your address), or pay twice for one score (each escrow UTXO can be spent exactly once, and the oracle refuses to attest a score it already paid).
  • What the oracle CAN do: lie about a score, or refuse to attest. That is the residual trust. In production the oracle would be run by FoldingCash itself (the same party that runs today's monthly distributions) — or by several independent signers with an m-of-n scheme.
  • Username binding: Folding@home usernames have no cryptography behind them — anyone can type someone else's name. The oracle attests the score for a name; binding a name to a person is the same registration trust FoldingCash already uses for its distributions. A stronger binding (e.g. proving control of a Folding@home passkey) is future work, not claimed here.
  • Demo economics: 1 FOLD per 100,000 Folding@home points, capped at 50,000 FOLD per claim. Real FLDCH distribution rates are set by FoldingCash; this rate is a demo stand-in.
  • What is verified on-chain: oracle signature over the claim (username, amount, score, nonce), claimant signature, payout address covenant, single-spend escrow. Every funding transaction carries an OP_RETURN FoldClaim:<user>:<score>:<index> memo, so all attestations are auditable on-chain.

How it works

  1. You enter your Folding@home username. The app reads your score from the official stats API (proxied through the oracle — the API sends no CORS headers, so browsers cannot call it directly).
  2. The oracle signs a claim: nonce = sha256(claimantPk ‖ amount ‖ username ‖ score ‖ index), then funds a one-time escrow contract with your FOLD.
  3. Your browser builds the claim transaction: the contract checks the oracle signature (OP_CHECKDATASIG), your signature, and that the tokens go to your address — then releases the FOLD to you.
  4. Claim again only after you fold more: the oracle refuses to attest a score it already paid.
